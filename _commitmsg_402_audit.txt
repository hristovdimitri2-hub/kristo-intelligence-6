Self-audit against a published x402 failure taxonomy — we pass

A CDP-verified post published a census of 40,608 x402 resources on
4,313 hosts (87.8% clean) and named four defects by size. Three of
them are checkable on ourselves for free, so we checked, live.

We are in none of them: 7/7 advertised URLs answer 402, 7/7
resource.url equals the requested URL, all https, all offer a plain
"exact" scheme.

The class that mattered is the 788 that "name another URL in their
402 than the one requested", whose published fix is building
resource.url from the public origin and setting trust proxy behind a
TLS proxy. We sit behind Render's TLS proxy, so a missing proxy
handling would have made every 402 advertise http:// — and no local
test would catch it, because locally there is no proxy and the scheme
is right by accident. It is right in production; recorded so nobody
removes it.

Also verified in passing: OPTIONS 204 with the full CORS allowlist,
DELETE -> 405 with an Allow header and no accepts in the body (wrong
method refused before money), and two ASCII WWW-Authenticate lines
(x402 228 B, MPP Payment 547 B). The MPP rail is the riskier one —
the same census has MPP at 64.6% clean against 88% for x402.

Recorded as deliberate, not a defect: the triplicated accepts /
accepts[] / x402_accepts shim.

Two free options this opened are listed but NOT taken, because both
are external actions and wait for the owner's explicit approval: a
free full check by DM, and Fizzl's free hourly single-endpoint
monitor with alerts.
