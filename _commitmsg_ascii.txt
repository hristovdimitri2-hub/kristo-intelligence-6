fix(x402): the MPP challenge made the paid route unpayable — non-ASCII header

Caught by verifying the previous commit against the LIVE host instead of
trusting the local suite, and it is the reason that step exists.

What happened, from the deploy at 21:29:31Z:

    x402 payment required: endpoint=/api/v1/verify-seller, price=$0.01
    [WARNING] Invalid request from ip=10.195.135.23: Invalid HTTP Header:
      '\'Payment id="e9YDatXFTF3THluuzpPffbyVC5yhT3nT", realm="kristo-intelligence",
      method="evm", intent="charge", request="…", expires="…",
      header="PAYMENT-SIGNATURE", description="Verify an x402 seller before you
      pay it — a seller audit on chain: …"\'' 
    "GET /api/v1/verify-seller?receiver=0x…" 400 0

The em-dash came from OUR OWN selling copy. `CHALLENGE_DESCRIPTIONS` is written
in prose, and an HTTP field-value must be ASCII; gunicorn rejected the response
outright and the edge turned it into a 502. Net effect: adding the MPP challenge
to satisfy a catalogue WARNING made a PAID ROUTE UNPAYABLE — strictly worse than
the warning. The legacy `x402` token was never affected because it carries no
description, which is why every earlier probe of the 402 envelope was clean.

Fixed with `_header_safe`: fold the punctuation our prose actually uses
(em/en dash, curly quotes, ellipsis, arrows, ≥/≤, nbsp), drop whatever is still
outside ASCII, escape `\` and `"`, collapse whitespace, truncate. A description
is decoration and no decoration is worth an unpayable route. A final
`encode("ascii")` check drops the optional `description` parameter entirely
rather than let a bad header leave the process.

Three new tests, including one that walks EVERY paid endpoint and asserts every
response header is ASCII-encodable — the assertion that would have caught this
before it reached production. Suite 1346.

The other three fixes verified live and are working: OPTIONS now answers 204 with
`Access-Control-Allow-Methods` and `Access-Control-Allow-Headers` naming the
payment headers; POST to a GET-only paid route answers 405 `method_not_allowed`
with `payment_taken: false` and never reaches the settlement rail;
/api/dashboard/data answers 200.
